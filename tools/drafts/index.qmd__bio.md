I am currently a Data Scientist at 84.51 in Cincinnati, OH. I am on leave from my doctoral program in Economics at the University of Cincinnati after the birth of my son!

My interests lie at the confluence of economics, history and philosophy of science, and especially spatial issues; I am interested in the ways that how social scientists think about the world and study it comes to shape it, physically or otherwise. 

My professional career spans economic research, technical roles, K12 education (as a Teaching Fellow in the Alliance for Catholic Education). I have worked in math education and education research; tax policy, transit finance, natural language processing, and geospaital analysis to name a few.

However, throughout theses seemingly disparate topics, I have maintained my interest in the philosophical and historical roots of the way we research and understand ourselves; this research has been deeply shaped by my own Catholic faith and a variety of transformative seminars and personal experiences I have had the opportunity to participate in. In spite of these interests, often I find the most rhetorically convincing arguments to synthesize these philosophical concerns with rigorous statistical methods prized by social scientists (especially economists).

I have B.A.s in Economics and Mathematics from the University of Cincinnati, a Master of Educaiton from the University of Notre Dame, and am a Wolfgang-Mayer Fellow in Economics at the University of Cincinnati Carl H. Lindner College of Business.
